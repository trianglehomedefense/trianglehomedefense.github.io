We go where the animals go

The sound is only the beginning of the inspection.

Wildlife follows repeatable paths through rooflines, gable vents, soffits, attics, crawlspaces, foundations and utility gaps. We follow that route as far as the accessible conditions allow, photograph what matters and separate what we observed from what was reported.

When you call, start with the odd part: “scratching over the bedroom,” “bats leaving the gable,” “snake under the porch,” or “droppings by the crawlspace.” That is enough to start.

A simple route forward

Inspect. Remove. Repair. Remediate. Prevent.

Not every property needs every step. The inspection tells us which ones actually apply.

  1. 1. InspectFind evidence, travel routes, entry points and contributing conditions.
  2. 2. RemoveAddress active wildlife when appropriate, lawful and within the approved scope.
  3. 3. RepairCorrect observed access points with durable, animal-resistant materials.
  4. 4. RemediateAddress accessible debris, contamination or damaged material when included.
  5. 5. PreventReduce the conditions and openings that support repeat access.

Property-specific corrections

Fix the opening in a way that belongs on the house.

When repair work falls within our scope, we build the correction around the observed condition. The goal is to close the access point, restore the protective barrier and match the existing construction as closely as practical using durable, animal-resistant materials.

Quick answers before you call

Common wildlife questions in the Triangle

Can bats be excluded in North Carolina right now?

Yes, the statewide bat pup-rearing eviction moratorium runs May 1 through July 31. Beginning August 1, exclusion may resume when conditions and the approved scope allow. If you are seeing bats now, this is a useful time to identify active routes.

I heard scratching but do not know what animal it is. Should I still call?

Yes. Time of day, where the sound is located, visible droppings, chewing and roof or crawlspace openings can help narrow the concern. You do not need to diagnose it first.

What do you look for after a snake sighting?

We look at visible access points, crawlspace and foundation conditions, stored materials, vegetation and signs of prey pressure. The goal is to understand why the area is useful to the animal.

Do you serve Raleigh, Fuquay-Varina, Apex and Clayton?

Yes, those communities are within our Greater RDU service area. Priority coverage is south of Raleigh and along the US-1 corridor. Call or text and we will confirm availability before scheduling.
